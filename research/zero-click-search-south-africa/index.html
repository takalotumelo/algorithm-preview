    Zero-click search in South Africa

    What 16 months of Google Search Console data from 110 South African websites tells us about clicks, AI Overviews and organic search.

    Read the findingsHow we did it

    Every 100 appearances in Google

    For every 100 times these websites appeared in Google for a South African searcher, about 5 people clicked through. That's 67.3 million clicks from 1.43 billion appearances.

    01 · Why we ran this

    Why we ran this study

    Most of what gets written about zero-click search comes from the US and Europe, built on panels of browser data. South African marketers get those numbers second hand and have to guess how much of it applies here.

    We look after Google Search Console for more than 100 South African websites, from banks and retailers to manufacturers and schools. That gives us a first-party way to ask the question for this market: when a page shows up in Google for a South African searcher, how often does that person click, and is that changing?

    So we pulled every day of data Google still holds for every one of those sites, 19 May 2025 to 28 September 2026, and compared like with like.

    What this data can and can't tell you

    Search Console shows how often our clients' pages appeared in Google and how often they were clicked. It doesn't show what a searcher did instead, whether they clicked a competitor, an ad or nothing at all. So this report measures clicks per appearance for the sites in the study, which is the part of zero-click search a business can see in its own data and act on.

    Every figure is anonymised. No client, website or search term is named, a sector is only reported when at least 5 separate businesses sit in it, and the headline figures describe the typical business rather than a total the biggest sites would dominate.

    05 · Cleaning the data

    3 breaks in Google's data

    Search Console isn't a stable measuring tool. Google changed how it counted at least 3 times in these 16 months, and any one of those changes would make clicks look as if they had collapsed or recovered.

    Break 1

    1 to 2 July 2025

    Overnight, the typical site's average position moved from about 7.5 to about 20, and its click rate fell from about 4.6 to about 3 clicks per 100 appearances. The extra appearances sit deep in the results and bring no clicks, which points to automated tools loading results pages.

    Break 2

    10 to 15 September 2025

    It reversed when Google stopped serving 100 results a page to rank-tracking tools. Average position went back to about 7 to 8 and has stayed there.

    Break 3

    Mid-December 2025 to late April 2026

    Google reported a growing share of non-branded appearances at position 1, from about 10% to 24%, taken from positions 3 to 10 while total appearances stayed flat. It unwound between 16 and 28 April 2026. We don't know the cause.

    How we handled it

    Every year-on-year comparison uses the same weeks a year apart with both sides clean: 19 May to 30 June, and 15 to 28 September. Anything that depends on position uses June to August 2026, after the third break unwound. Figures that don't depend on position come from site totals, which the third break doesn't touch.

    13 · Summary

    What zero-click looks like in South Africa

    Across 110 websites and 1.43 billion appearances, the pattern is consistent. South African businesses are showing up in Google more than they were a year ago and getting fewer clicks from it. On mobile, the typical business appeared 22% more often and got 8% fewer clicks over the same 6 weeks, and clicks per appearance fell for more than 8 in 10 businesses.

    AI Overviews are a large part of it. They show on 70% of the non-branded page-one search terms we checked, and the terms that carry one lost clicks faster than the terms that don't.

    Terms without an AI Overview lost clicks too, branded searches are losing clicks, and every sector we can report on is down, so AI Overviews explain part of the change and not all of it.

    Organic search still delivered 67.3 million clicks to these websites over the 16 months. What has changed is how many appearances it takes to earn one, and that's what we now plan and report against.

    One-page brief for marketing teams · October 2026

    What South African marketers should do about zero-click search

    South African businesses are being seen more in Google and clicked less. The typical business went from 4.8 to 3.0 clicks per 100 appearances in a year, AI Overviews show on 70% of non-branded page-one searches, and even branded searches, which bring in 75% of the typical business's clicks, lost 16% of their clicks per appearance.

    Paid media

    What it means: organic clicks are falling on the searches you also pay for, and Search Console can't show where they went.

    • Re-test bidding on your own name. You may now be paying for branded visits you used to get free. Switch brand ads off in some regions for a few weeks (a holdout test) and measure what you lose.
    • Cover comparison searches. "Best", "vs" and review searches lost the most organic clicks per appearance, 34%. Your ads can show above or below the AI Overview on them.
    • Get ready for ads inside AI Overviews. Google already runs them in 12 countries, including Kenya and Nigeria, but not South Africa yet (October 2026). They'll come from your existing Search, Shopping and Performance Max campaigns, and you can't opt out.
    • Redo the budget maths. Model organic with fewer clicks per appearance and track blended cost per acquisition, so a fall in free clicks doesn't become a paid budget increase nobody planned.

    Organic as a system

    What it means: you can be quoted by Google's AI, named by ChatGPT or found on TikTok without anyone visiting your website, so organic has to be planned and measured as one system.

    • Put it in one plan with one owner. Plan SEO, AEO, GEO and social search together, because the same content and authority work feeds all of them.
    • Report visibility next to traffic. Put appearances, clicks, AI Overview citations and AI engine mentions side by side, because traffic on its own hides a rise in visibility.

    AEO and GEO

    What it means: AEO gets you quoted in Google's AI Overviews, and GEO gets you recommended when someone asks ChatGPT, Gemini or Perplexity.

    • Find out where you stand. Check which of your 50 most valuable searches show an AI Overview and whether you're quoted. Then ask the AI engines your customers' questions and note who gets named.
    • Write so a machine can quote you. Answer the question in the first 2 lines, keep prices, specs and locations in text rather than images, add structured data, and keep your facts the same on every site that lists you.
    • Earn mentions on other websites. AI engines recommend the businesses the web talks about, so get into the reviews, press, comparison articles and directories they cite for your category.

    Traditional SEO

    What it means: traditional search delivered 67.3 million clicks to the websites in this study over 16 months, and Google's AI Overviews are built from the same index, so it's still the foundation.

    • Fix the technical basics first. A page Google can't crawl or index can't rank or be quoted. Some security settings now block AI crawlers by default, so check yours.
    • Put effort into pages that earn the click. People still click for prices and stock, products, tools and calculators, local detail and original data.
    • Compare like with like. Use the same weeks a year apart, read mobile on its own, and allow for the 3 breaks in Search Console's data since July 2025, described in section 05 of the report.

    15 · Method

    How we did it, and the limits

    Data sourceGoogle Search Console Search Analytics for web search, filtered to searchers in South Africa, read through Algorithm's own Search Console access. One property per website: where a website had several, the one with the most South African appearances.

    Websites110 websites run by 71 businesses in 8 sectors, which is every website we could read with any South African data. Each business's websites are combined before any figure is worked out.

    Window19 May 2025 to 28 Sep 2026, 498 days. That's every day Google still held when we pulled the data on 1 October 2026; Google keeps 16 months.

    Search termsSearch Console only names the search terms enough people use. For the typical business about 65% of appearances come with a named search term. Site totals include everything, so headline figures come from totals and search-term figures are used for the splits.

    Branded searchesMatched against each business's brand and product names, including common misspellings, plus login and portal searches.

    Types of searchQuestions start with how, what, why and similar words. Comparison and buying searches contain words like price, best, buy, review or near me. Funnel stages use DataForSEO's search intent labels: informational, commercial, transactional and navigational.

    Typical businessThe median business. A business only counts towards a figure with at least 1 000 appearances in it (500 for the same-weeks comparisons and 300 per group for the AI Overview comparisons).

    Same weeks a year apart19 May to 30 June and 15 to 28 September, 2025 against 2026, worked out per day so the windows match.

    Breaks in Google's data3, described in section 05: 1 July to 14 September 2025 is left out of every comparison, and comparisons by position avoid December 2025 to May 2026.

    AI OverviewsDataForSEO Labs Keyword Overview, location South Africa, language English, with results page data. Up to 300 non-branded page-one search terms per business, each with at least 30 appearances from June to September 2026. 11 811 terms checked, 9 419 in DataForSEO's database, 5 850 with a results page captured from June 2026 onwards; older captures are left out.

    AnonymityNo business, website or search term is named. A sector is only reported with at least 5 businesses in it, headline figures are the typical business rather than totals, and sector figures are rates and changes, never volumes.

    Limits

    • These are the websites we work on, not every South African search, and the mix leans towards the sectors we work in.
    • Appearances aren't searches. When 2 of these websites show on the same results page, that's 2 appearances from 1 search.
    • Search Console doesn't show what a searcher did instead of clicking, so this report can't say how many searches ended with no click at all.
    • Desktop appearances look inflated by automated traffic since September 2025, which is why we lead with mobile.
    • Sector groups are small, from 5 to 25 businesses, so sector figures show direction rather than precise benchmarks.
    • The AI Overview check is one results page per search term, not a history, and only covers terms in DataForSEO's database.
    • Google changed its counting at least 3 times in the window. We've kept comparisons clear of each change, but there may be smaller ones we haven't caught.
